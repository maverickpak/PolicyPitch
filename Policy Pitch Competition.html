Introduction

The Department of Strategic Studies is launching the first-ever Policy Pitch Workshop and Competition at the National Defence University. This initiative aims to strengthen analytical skills, strategic thinking, and the ability to convert complex national security challenges into actionable policy solutions.

Theme for 2025

“Strategic Stability in South Asia Amid Emerging India–Pakistan Conflict Pathways: Policy Designs for a Turbulent Future”

Participants will explore future conflict scenarios shaped by nuclear modernisation, cyber threats to NC2, AI-enabled targeting systems, hypersonics, economic coercion, and crisis misinformation. Policy solutions must be practical, feasible, and strategically relevant.

Who Can Participate

  • All students enrolled in the Department of Strategic Studies, NDU.
  • Team size: 2–4 members.
  • Interdepartmental teams allowed.

Event Structure

Stage 1: Registration (Week 1)

Teams form, select sub-theme, and submit registration form.

Stage 2: Concept Note Submission (Week 2)

One-page document outlining the problem, evidence, and preliminary policy direction.

Stage 3: Shortlisting (Week 3)

Top teams selected based on clarity, relevance, and feasibility.

Stage 4: Policy Brief Submission (Week 4)

Teams submit a 2–3 page policy brief following the provided template.

Stage 5: Policy Pitch Workshop (Week 5)

Training session covering problem identification, argument structuring, evidence development, and pitch delivery.

Stage 6: Final Event (Week 6)

Teams deliver a 7-minute pitch followed by 3-minute Q&A.

Pitch Slide Requirements

  • Maximum 6 slides.
  • Mandatory structure: Title, Problem, Evidence, Policy, Implementation, Impact.
  • Font size minimum 24.
  • No long paragraphs; use visuals where possible.

Sub-Themes

  • India’s counterforce trajectory and implications for deterrence.
  • Cyber threats to nuclear command and control.
  • AI-enabled targeting and autonomous platforms.
  • Hypersonics and cross-domain escalation.
  • Space vulnerabilities in future conflict scenarios.
  • Information warfare and crisis misinterpretation.
  • Maritime competition and choke-point vulnerabilities.
  • Economic coercion and regional strategic pressures.

Awards

  • Letters of Participation for all shortlisted teams.
  • Letters of Appreciation for top three teams.
  • Winning policy brief may be published in departmental newsletter or presented to faculty panel

FAQs

Is this competition only for Strategic Studies students? Yes. All students from any program being offered by the department of Strategic Studies only may participate.

Is prior experience required? No, the workshop prepares beginners.

How much time does it require? 2–3 hours per week for 3–4 weeks.

Are workshops mandatory? Yes, for shortlisted teams.

Can a team submit more than one concept note? No. Only one submission per team.
